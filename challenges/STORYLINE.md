# FLAG THEFT AUTO — The Los Santos Files

## 00:00 — The Phone Rings

A radio crackles inside an abandoned garage on the edge of Los Santos. The city's digital underworld is in chaos: a rogue dispatch network has hidden twenty-five clearance fragments across police case files, airport transmissions, black-market packages, abandoned relays, and a military gate. The elusive Broker has contracted your crew to recover the fragments before the citywide network lockdown.

Your crew is **not** breaking into real banks or computers. This is a fictional, offline evidence hunt: every mission has a self-contained, authorized digital artifact, and the only heist is against a puzzle.

The Broker's last instruction is simple: **“Bring me the flags. Leave every real system untouched.”**

## Act I — The Street Job

Your getaway car needs a route. First you must piece together Lester's intercepted pager, decode a Vinewood taxi message, inspect a hidden safehouse stash, read the Del Perro camera record, and discover a false-file-extension trick at Los Santos Customs. From there, decode a payphone's split tone burst, key a Vinewood wanted poster, reassemble a dead-drop uplink, crack a chop-shop terminal's obfuscated checker, ignite the towing-yard bytecode ignition, and reassemble the Ghost Relay broadcast plus its linked Relay Archive. These twelve street missions introduce cryptography, steganography, and evidence inspection. Each successful extraction earns **50 points**.

*Radio call:* “The city is wide open. Start small. The easy money is in the details nobody checks.”

## Act II — The Big Heist

The crew's operation grows more dangerous—but remains completely fictional. Use a local mock Fleeca Bank kiosk with intentionally faulty SQL, trace a ghost aircraft's HTTP dispatch in a captured airport PCAP, reconstruct an LSPD evidence log, decrypt an Underground Market radio message, and repair a damaged Python courier. Then take on the Nightshift trilogy: unpack a private-loader implant, recover its smeared configuration, and emulate its stateful command-and-control protocol. Eight middle-tier missions award **100 points** each.

*Radio call:* “Every camera, every packet, every line of code tells a different story. Find the one that's true.”

## Act III — The Final Getaway

The fictional authorities have closed the freeways. Crack a Fort Zancudo Linux gate binary, calculate a recursive freeway clearance, peel layers from an impound-camera archive, review a deliberately unsafe local authorization gateway, and break the final four-party escape agreement. Five high-tier missions award **160 points** each.

*Radio call:* “Forget the wanted stars. The only thing that matters now is your score. Get in, find the flags, get out.”

## Victory Condition

Recover as many of the **twenty-five** unique `FTA{...}` messages as possible, submit them to CTFd under your crew's account before the public deadline, and climb the leaderboard. Full completion totals **2,200 points**. Every crew has access to three progressive hints per mission; unlocking a hint deducts points from your crew's score, and three wrong flag submissions lock that mission's terminal for 120 seconds.

Missions are independent **except** the Nightshift trilogy (M06 → M07 → M08), which is sequential: each part recovers a token the next part requires.

**This is an original educational cyber-noir storyline inspired by open-world crime-game aesthetics, not affiliated with the creators of Grand Theft Auto.**
